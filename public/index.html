UNDER THE BODYWORK

Pick it up.
See what moves.

Use the left and right arrows below the car to browse ten vehicle setups. Each has its own body, weight, tyres, suspension travel, spring rates and drive layout. Cutaway reveals the linkages; drag the ground to view them from any angle.

Drag the car itself: pull a front or rear corner to tilt that corner, or pull the middle to lift the whole body while holding it level. Grab a wheel to lift its hub and watch the suspension compress and articulate. Drag the body down to compress the suspension and release to watch it bounce. Drag the ground to orbit. A subtle outline shows the area under your pointer; there are no permanent handles. The camera follows horizontally while keeping its height fixed. For keyboard lifting, Tab to an area, hold Shift and press Up or Down, then release. You can grab the body or a wheel while moving: the lift follows the car horizontally, and the arrow keys keep working.

The chassis has front, centre and rear sections connected by elastic joints. Wheel carriers, suspension arms and solid axles attach to the section that supports them. Struts slide and pivot, wishbones hinge, and locating links or leaf springs constrain the live axles. Springs, dampers, travel limits and ground contact act on those bodies.

Body flex: the structure bends and twists under suspension and ground loads, then springs back when released. The pickup and off-road frames are more compliant; the road cars use stiffer unibody settings. The pickup cab and bed follow their supporting sections, while the frame can flex beneath them. Try Pickup, Cutaway and ¼×, then lift one wheel. TWIST measures the front-to-rear angle of the chassis; it is separate from the whole car leaning on its suspension. The visible motion is shown at actual scale.

Compact, hatchback and minivan: front MacPherson struts and rear torsion beams. Sedan, wagon and coupe: front struts and independent five-link rear suspension. Sports car and SUV: front double wishbones and five-link rear suspension. Pickup: front wishbones and a leaf-sprung rear live axle. Off-road 4×4: two coil-sprung live axles with triangulated four-link location.

The car bodies, glass, lights and wheels come from Generic passenger car pack by Comrade1280, adapted under CC BY 4.0. We separated and normalized the models, centred their wheel pivots and added the simulated undercarriages. Body proportions and tyre dimensions follow the meshes. Upper mounts are fitted to each bonnet and rear wheelhouse. Inner wheel arches are recessed to allow the tyres to tuck underneath the original outer fenders; front and rear compression and droop limits are tuned separately. Engines, power ratings, gear ratios, suspension layouts, mass and rates are representative choices for these fictional designs, not measured production-car specifications.

Drive with the arrow keys: ↑ accelerates forward, ↓ brakes and then reverses, and ← / → steer. The automatic gearbox changes gear for you; the small readout shows gear and RPM. Release the keys to coast: rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag and engine braking slow the car. The electric sedan uses one forward gear and regenerative slowing. There is no traction control or ABS, so wheelspin and locked brakes remain possible. The on-screen arrows work for touch too. Speed measures movement across the ground in any direction, even while sliding or tumbling; vertical falls do not add to it. Opening the page or pressing Reset starts the car 70 cm above the ground, so you can watch it land and bounce; ¼× slows both driving and suspension motion.

Choose Flat for smooth asphalt, Gravel for small corrugations and looser grip, or Rough trail for larger rounded bumps and dips. The moving grid follows the actual road shape. Try the same terrain at the same speed in different cars; changing a setup or road starts a fresh drop. Terrain contact physically pushes each wheel through its suspension. The same tyre model remains active at rest: its friction resists wheel scrub during compression and rebound. It includes wheel inertia, longitudinal slip, a transient lateral brush response, load-sensitive grip and a combined-slip friction limit. Contact forces pass through the suspension, loading the outside tyres and rolling the body. Hard braking or acceleration leaves less grip for cornering. The bars show tyre loads while driving and wheel travel from full droop before driving.

Sound adds soft damper movement, suspension travel stops, landing thumps, tyre rolling and friction during scrubbing or sliding. It follows wheel contact, load and movement; orbit to hear each side, and try ¼× to slow the sounds with the motion. Sound starts only when you turn it on. Combustion engines use real recordings blended with RPM, load and gear changes. Sliding tyres use a quiet, softened recording. The electric sedan has a quiet motor whine and is silent at rest.

Engine and skid recordings are adapted from public CC0 / CC BY sources; audio credits and licences. These are representative sound families for the fictional cars. Suspension, landing, rolling and electric motor sounds remain procedural; the mix is illustrative, not a calibrated acoustic model.

An educational model with illustrative tyre and surface parameters, not a validated vehicle or measured tyre dataset. Front steering uses virtual knuckles and Ackermann angles; the visible tie rods follow that steering. Leaf bending uses effective half-leaves, shackles and a vertical spring law; the torsion beam uses coupled trailing arms. Chassis flex is a reduced elastic model with representative stiffness and damping, not measured material or weld data. Joint limits keep it within an elastic demonstration range; it does not simulate yielding, panel buckling, crash damage or detailed structural stress. Tyre carcass deformation, camber thrust and self-aligning torque are omitted. Gravel uses a continuous corrugated surface; individual loose stones, deformable soil and tyre enveloping are omitted. Very short wavelengths are limited by the 25 cm terrain mesh.
